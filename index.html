His life

Remembering Jerry

Jerry Don Rucker, former Dallas city councilman, lawyer, businessman and lifelong raconteur, died peacefully in Dallas on November 2, 2026, aged 84.

Born to Jerry Ross Rucker and Lola Dean Flinn in Dallas on January 10, 1942, Rucker grew up in modest circumstances in Dallas and Grand Saline, Texas. He graduated from Southern Methodist University and its law school before serving as a Navy lawyer during the Vietnam War.

Returning to Dallas, he became a trial lawyer, construction executive and businessman. As a principal of Rucker Construction, he participated in the building of major public and commercial facilities across Texas. He later founded General American Fidelity and Guaranty, an insurance company, and helped shape regional transportation and development planning.

Elected to the Dallas City Council in 1983, he served six years as an at-large representative, earning a reputation for intelligence, wit and a spectacular disregard for political diplomacy. D Magazine dubbed him “King Ruck,” portraying a man as capable of captivating a room as exasperating his colleagues. He served as president of the North Central Texas Council of Governments and vice-chairman of the Regional Transportation Council, and ran unsuccessfully for Congress in 1990.

His interests and ambitions rarely observed conventional boundaries. Before becoming a lawyer, he sang in a rock-and-roll band. He enjoyed hunting, watching football and the company of friends, preferably with a story to tell or an argument to pursue. He was also deeply attached to the dogs that accompanied him through life.

Jerry never gave much thought to growing old, and showed little inclination to accommodate it when it arrived. Dysphagia eventually quieted a man who had spent much of his life commanding conversations, but did little to alter his personality. He remained sociable, mischievous, opinionated and impatient with rules, charming and exasperating those around him in much the same measure as he had throughout his life.

He spent many years in Houston with his second wife, Caroline, who died in September 2026. He is survived by his brother Pat, his first wife Andria, his sons, Kurt and Austin, and his grandchildren, Kai and Rowan.

In notes prepared for his own obituary, he offered a characteristically concise assessment of his life:

“Life is short, the tasks and duties are long.”

And, with apparent satisfaction:

“Thanks to all for a busy and involving run of it.”
